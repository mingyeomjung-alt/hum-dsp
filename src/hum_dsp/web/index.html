Resampling and channel handling

When any band is enabled, 44.1 kHz input is resampled to 48 kHz with a fixed FIR filter, processed, and returned to its original sample rate. With all bands at 0 dB, it stays unchanged without resampling. The channel count is preserved; there is no automatic level normalization.

For stereo, settings estimated from the channel with the higher whole-file RMS are applied to both channels. L and R are not summed. Preservation of wanted sound and improved audio quality are not guaranteed.

M4A, MP3, 24-bit WAV and floating-point WAV are not supported in this mode.

Explicit offline persistent-hum candidate

Supports native 44.1/48 kHz mono or stereo PCM16, PCM24 and Float32 WAV, up to 60 seconds or 128 MiB. It requires at least four stable harmonic lines before applying a shared whole-file spectral mask. Insufficient evidence leaves the validated audio payload unchanged; that is an abstention, not successful removal.

The analysis does not know whether a harmonic line is noise. Wanted sound in the same 50/60 Hz harmonic family can also be reduced. Full-file lookahead and FFT workspaces use memory; this is not streaming, microphone processing or real-time cancellation. There is no automatic normalization, clipping, quality pass or perceptual approval.

Input and result copies are temporary and are replaced by the next Long WAV, High-resolution WAV, MP3/M4A or Persistent hum job, or removed on normal processor shutdown. Save files you need first. Forced termination may leave copies behind; secure erasure is not guaranteed.

Guard behavior and limitations

Only bands supported by the declared interval are candidates. If amplitude exceeds twice the reference anywhere in the file, that entire band is left untreated. Growing noise may also remain. This offline analysis reads future portions of the file; it is not real-time protection.

Equal-amplitude overlapping wanted sound, a mistaken noise-only declaration and frequency changes may not be adequately protected. Signal restoration, preservation of wanted content and improved audio quality are not guaranteed. This mode accepts only 48 kHz mono PCM16 WAV up to 60 seconds.

Understand the current limits.
Wanted sound at a selected frequency may also be reduced. Per-band control is manual: you choose each attenuation or disable a band; it does not automatically prevent damage. Existing tracking is limited to ±1 Hz and 0.05 Hz/s at the fundamental, scaled by harmonic order in harmonic and per-band modes. Guarded, harmonic and per-band processing begin after about 2 seconds of observation; release is delayed after noise ends. Noise-profile mode applies ±1 Hz and 0.05 Hz/s to each individual harmonic band and reads future portions of the entire file. Persistent hum is a separate explicit whole-file candidate: it requires four stable harmonic lines, can damage wanted same-family content, and may abstain without changing the payload. Neither experimental mode supplies an automatic quality pass. There is no broadband denoising, real-time microphone processing or spatial noise cancellation.
